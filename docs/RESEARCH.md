# What the research says — and what EyeTime does with it

EyeTime's productivity features are chosen by evidence, and this page is honest about how strong that evidence is. "Strong" = replicated experiments or large meta-analyses; "Moderate" = solid single studies or mixed replication; "Weak" = observational data, small samples or mostly popular-press support. Nothing below is medical or psychological advice.

| Idea | What is known | Evidence | In EyeTime |
|---|---|---|---|
| **If-then plans** (implementation intentions) | Deciding in advance "if X happens, then I will Y" helps people act on intentions. A 2006 review (94 tests) found d ≈ 0.65; a 2024 update of 642 tests found smaller, still positive effects (≈ .27–.66), larger for if-then format, motivated people and rehearsed plans. ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f), verified against the record; the 2024 update by Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer is cited from a secondary summary and not independently checked) | Strong | **Pause page shows your own plan** ("If I want to open reddit.com, then I will stand up and drink water"); tasks can be **scheduled for a time** (the "when"), which fires a notification that starts the session. |
| **Attention residue** | After switching tasks, part of your attention stays on the unfinished one, hurting the next task — most when work is unfinished, interrupted or you expect to rush later. Leroy & Glomb proposed a "ready to resume" plan. ([Leroy 2009](https://www.uwb.edu/business/faculty/sophie-leroy/attention-residue)) | Moderate | **"Where did you leave off?"** after each session and in the evening; the note appears under the task and when you start it again. **Site switches per hour** shows how fragmented your day is. |
| **Plans quiet unfinished goals** | Unfulfilled goals intrude on unrelated tasks; making a *specific plan* removes much of that interference, even before the goal is done. (Masicampo & Baumeister 2011, JPSP; read via secondary summaries) | Moderate | **Close the day**: one win, resume notes, up to three priorities for tomorrow — tomorrow's plan is applied automatically in the morning. **Park a thought** during a session and **Park it for later** on the pause page. |
| **Small wins and daily review** | In ~12,000 diary entries from 238 people, making progress on meaningful work was the strongest driver of good days; Amabile & Kramer suggest a daily review and choosing one next action. ([summary](https://bigthink.com/articles/progress-principle/)) | Moderate | Goal progress and **goal streak**, "held back N distractions", a **wins log**, and an evening review. Days off do not break the streak. |
| **Micro-breaks** | A 2022 meta-analysis (22 samples, N = 2,335) found small but real gains in vigor (d = .36) and less fatigue (d = .35); performance gains were small and non-significant (d = .16), larger for longer breaks. ([Albulescu et al. 2022](https://doaj.org/article/c6f37b6ba159469296b2cb968b65505f)) | Moderate | Short breaks after each session, a **long break every Nth session**, and concrete **break ideas** (stand, look far, water, breathe) instead of a bare countdown; 20-20-20 eye breaks. Breaks are pitched as recovery, not as a performance hack. |
| **"Ideal" focus intervals** | The 52/17 figure comes from one company's tracking data and was revised later; evidence for Pomodoro-style timing is thin and mostly from student samples. Neither is clearly better. ([DeskTime](https://desktime.com/blog/52-17)) | Weak | No dogma: EyeTime offers 15/25/50/90 and **learns your best length** from your own session ratings (needs enough rated sessions at two or more lengths, and a clear winner). |
| **Fast context switching** | Gloria Mark's logging studies report that time on one screen before switching fell from ~2.5 minutes (2004) to ~47 seconds (since ~2016; median 40 s) — a description of behaviour, not a biological limit. A frequently quoted "~25 minutes to refocus" figure appears in interviews, but we could not find the underlying study, so **EyeTime does not use it**. ([Steelcase interview](https://www.steelcase.com/research/articles/our-47-second-attention-span-with-gloria-mark-s5-ep3-transcript/)) | Moderate (47 s) / unverified (25 min) | Site switches per hour in Insights, "what pulls you away" (temptations per site), and a counter of distractions held back during sessions. |
| **Blocking vs. reminders** | In student workshops, blocking tools were rated most useful and goal reminders least; nudges alone seem to do little for screen time. Rigorous effect sizes for blockers are still missing. (Lyngs and colleagues, CHI 2024 — known to us only through search summaries, not read in full) | Weak–moderate | Real friction stays: pause page, daily limits, schedules, strict sessions and **"only my sites"** scope. The *reason chips* and *if-then plan* are there so the friction is a decision, not a wall. |
| **Ambient sound** | Evidence is mixed and individual; moderate noise can help some people and hurt others. | Weak | Optional, off by default: synthesised brown noise / rain / café — plays only during a session. |

## Principles we took from this

1. **Plan the response, not just the goal.** If-then plans and "ready to resume" notes beat willpower.
2. **Close loops.** Unfinished things nag; a concrete next step quiets them.
3. **Make progress visible, keep it kind.** Small wins and days off beat punishing streaks.
4. **Learn from the person, not the average.** Where the literature disagrees (intervals, sound), EyeTime measures *your* ratings.
5. **Say what we do not know.** Unverified numbers do not go in the product.

Everything is computed locally; ratings, wins and plans never leave your device.

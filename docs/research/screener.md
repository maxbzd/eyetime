# Who to invite

## The mix (18 invited, 15 or more sessions held)

EyeTime is for people who work on a screen and want to keep their attention and their eyes in order. Not everybody who works on a screen is the same user, so the sample is deliberately mixed:

| Group | Count | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge workers (office, remote, freelancers) | 6 | The core. The new tab is their daily surface |
| Students (university or serious self-study) | 4 | Many starters, little money for tools, strong need for focus |
| Developers / designers | 3 | They will notice everything, including settings and shortcuts |
| People who say they struggle to focus (ADHD, "I lose hours") | 3 | The people the quiet design must not fail |
| People who use no productivity tools at all | 2 | The test of "understandable without the vocabulary" |

Mix of languages: at least 4 sessions in the language that is not English (the ones we have: ru, es, de, fr, pt, it, pl, uk, zh, ja). Mix of browsers' knowledge: at least 3 people who have never installed a browser extension themselves (they can be helped to *open* the pre-installed browser, nothing else).
Mix of machines: at least 4 on a laptop screen smaller than 1440 px wide; at least 3 who use light and 3 who use dark mode.

**Not invited:** anyone who works on a competing product, anyone who has seen EyeTime, anyone from the team's own circle (friends flatter). Maximum one person per household or company.

## The questions (five, in a form or a message — selects without revealing the product)

1. What do you do for work or study? *(select the groups above)*
2. How many hours a day are you in front of a screen? *(≥ 4 required)*
3. Which of these do you use to help you work or focus? *(a list including "none"; tools named, so we know what they compare with)*
4. Which browser do you use most? *(Chrome, Edge or Firefox; required: the build under test)*
5. Have you heard of or used "EyeTime"? *(no, required)*

Do **not** say what the product does before the session ("a tool to help focus"): say "a browser extension about how you use your computer".

## Recruiting

- Where: a short post in the communities the product already lives in, a message to the testers who gave feedback before, a student group, a freelancers' community, a research panel if a budget exists.
- Thank-you: a real amount (suggestion: 20–30 minutes of someone's time is worth a gift card of the local equivalent of 15–20 USD, or a donation of that size to a charity of their choice).
- Confirm every session the day before, in the same words, with the time zone and a link or a place. Plan for one no-show in five.
